Stage 1, revision B10 · feasibility

Best-Vastu straight stair, space used efficiently

One straight stair in a single 1 m lane along the south wall, climbing west as Vastu prefers, with the laundry and storage underneath. No U or L turns and no second stair. An architect still needs to confirm the small-plot setbacks and the ground-coverage cap, and a structural engineer must design the frame after a soil test.

What changed in B10. The stair now climbs towards the west, the best Vastu direction, in the south of the plot. It is still one straight run per floor (17 risers with a rest landing midway), stacked directly above itself, so it takes only a single 1 m lane. You enter at the front through the main door in the auspicious west padas, walk along a locked entry hall beside the stair, and turn round onto the first step. You arrive at the front of each floor facing west and turn right (clockwise) into the room.

The laundry sits under the high end of the stair by the main door, with a cupboard under the rest landing and drawers under the low end. The overhead tank is back on the stair cabin in the roof's south-west corner.

To use the space well, bedroom 1 on the first floor now sleeps head-east with a 1.45 m wardrobe and loft along the north wall, which frees room for a proper living room with the TV between two north windows and a 1.6 m sofa. The second floor keeps the family lounge, the master suite, and the guest room / study with its ensuite.

Interior palette. The walkthrough now uses the recommended warm Indian–Australian palette with an Italian touch: warm cream walls (#F3EBDD), ivory ceilings, one teak wood tone, cream terrazzo with sage and terracotta chips in shared spaces and on the stair, pale yellow oxide in the bedrooms, pale sage kitchen uppers, olive upholstery, a honed tan granite counter and bronze fittings. The full interior design, with flooring prices and a budget, is in the separate interior design doc.

Latest change. The first-floor bathroom is now inside bedroom 1 (north-west corner, Vastu-preferred for toilets), and the bedroom shifted west into space the hall did not need. The living room grew from about 2.3 × 2.9 m to 3.3 × 2.9 m, enough for a 2.0 m three-seater, an armchair and a coffee table facing a 50" TV. Bedroom 1 now has a 4.5 ft double bed (head east) and opens onto the loggia; guests reach the bathroom through the bedroom. The stair sides are short rounded walls with a handrail on top instead of black grills.

3. Structure

This is a preliminary scheme so the layout is buildable. A licensed structural engineer must design and sign the actual drawings after a soil test.

ElementProposalWhy
FrameRCC frame: 8 columns about 230 × 380 mm, in two rows (north and south walls) at about 3.7 m spacing, running unbroken from footing to roof.No floating columns. Every column stacks straight down. Shown on all four plans.
Beams and slabsCross beams about 230 × 450–500 mm spanning the 5.4 m width at each column line. 150 mm two-way slabs. Edge beams around the stair opening.The car porch and open first floor stay free of middle columns.
FoundationsIsolated footings for the north row. Combined or strap footings for the south row, because those columns sit on the boundary and can't spread onto the neighbour's land.Eccentric boundary footings are the usual cause of tilting and cracks on narrow plots if not tied back.
StairsRCC waist-slab flights spanning between floor and rest-landing beams, carried by the south-wall columns and a 115–150 mm RCC spine wall between the two stair lanes.Each flight has the same geometry, so formwork can be reused.
Walls230 mm outer walls. Light AAC-block or drywall partitions wherever a wall above does not sit on a wall or beam below, for example the second-floor ensuite walls.Keeps point loads off slabs and avoids slab cracking.
Roof gardenDesign the roof slab for an extra 3–4 kN/m² of soil, plus a root-barrier waterproofing membrane, a protection screed, drainage cells and two outlets. Raised planters sit on beam lines.Leaks and overloading are the common roof-garden failures.
Codes and materialsIS 456 and IS 875 loads, IS 1893 (Bengaluru is seismic zone II) with IS 13920 detailing for the stilt floor, M25 concrete, Fe 500D/550D steel, a plinth beam, damp-proof course, anti-termite treatment and 40 mm cover at the plinth.Stilt floors are the soft storey in an earthquake, so columns need proper ductile detailing.
WaterSlope the roof and setbacks to drains. Fit a BWSSB rainwater-harvesting pit in the rear setback and a waterproofed sump in the north-east.Damp and seepage are the main long-term problems in Bengaluru homes.

5. Compromises to accept or reject

  • You walk about 5 m to the stair foot, along the locked entry hall on the ground floor and the hall on each floor above. That is the price of a single straight, west-climbing, stacked stair; it saves about 5 m² per floor over two side-by-side flights.
  • Bedroom 1 is compact, with a 4.5 ft double bed (head east), and the first floor's only bathroom is inside it, so guests go through the bedroom.
  • The master bedroom is about 9.3 m² and sits towards the north-centre, not the south-west.
  • The guest ensuite is in the south-east, a Vastu compromise.
  • The lounge glass faces west. Use low-e glass, openable panes and blinds or bamboo chiks.
  • Ground coverage may exceed a cap. See section 4.
