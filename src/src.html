Stage 1, revision B7 · feasibility

The stair moves to the front

The stair is now a U-turn at the front corner of every floor, so you never walk through a floor to reach the next flight. The plan still meets the main Vastu rules and keeps a simple, stackable frame. An architect still needs to confirm the small-plot setbacks and the ground-coverage cap, and a structural engineer must design the frame after a soil test.

What changed in B7. The stair is a stacked U-turn (dog-leg) in the front south-west corner. The main door now opens at the front, 2.4 m from the gate, straight onto the first flight. Each storey climbs east along one lane, turns at a half landing and comes back west along the south wall. You arrive facing the front, turn right into the floor, and the next flight starts from the same landing.

Because the stair no longer ends at the back of each floor, the rooms were re-planned. Bedroom 2 moved down to the north-east of the first floor and shares the front bathroom, which is now a full bathroom with a shower. The first-floor study went to make room for it. The second floor is now the family lounge plus one large master suite (about 21 m²) with its ensuite. The stilt gained a bike bay and workshop behind the stair, and the laundry sits inside the locked foyer.

The open stair side is a screen of slim black-steel balusters with a timber handrail, there are handrails on both sides of every flight, and a skylight still lights the stairwell. Minimum headroom improved from 2.26 m to 2.85 m.

Cinematic tour. The Walk tab now has a Cinematic tour button (or press C). It walks an automatic, smooth route through all 23 stops with warmer lighting, a wider-lens look, letterbox bars and room captions, so you can screen-record it (Cmd + Shift + 5 on a Mac). The 3D model is also available as an OBJ file for Twinmotion, D5 Render, Lumion or Blender, for photo-real renders.

1. Brief as now understood

  • Chikkalasandra, Bengaluru · A-khata · 20 × 40 ft (6.10 × 12.19 m, 74.3 m²) · west-facing, road on the west.
  • Stilt: parking, a short planted porch, and the main door at the front opening onto the stair. Laundry in the foyer, bike bay behind.
  • First floor: loggia, living room, pooja corner, open kitchen with an island sink, bedroom 2 and a common bathroom.
  • Second floor: an enclosed family lounge for everyone and a large master suite with an ensuite.
  • Roof: garden, drying deck, solar.
  • A U-turn stair at the front, stacked on every floor. Follow Hindu Vastu and make the structure sound.

3. Structure

This is a preliminary scheme so the layout is buildable. A licensed structural engineer must design and sign the actual drawings after a soil test.

ElementProposalWhy
FrameRCC frame: 8 columns about 230 × 380 mm, in two rows (north and south walls) at about 3.7 m spacing, running unbroken from footing to roof.No floating columns. Every column stacks straight down. Shown on all four plans.
Beams and slabsCross beams about 230 × 450–500 mm spanning the 5.4 m width at each column line. 150 mm two-way slabs. Edge beams around the stair opening.The car porch and open first floor stay free of middle columns.
FoundationsIsolated footings for the north row. Combined or strap footings for the south row, because those columns sit on the boundary and can't spread onto the neighbour's land.Eccentric boundary footings are the usual cause of tilting and cracks on narrow plots if not tied back.
StairsA stacked dog-leg core: RCC waist-slab flights spanning from floor beams to half-landing beams, with a 115–150 mm RCC spine wall between the flights and an RCC wall on the east side of the core.Every storey repeats the same geometry, so formwork can be reused. The core walls also stiffen the stilt floor against sway.
Walls230 mm outer walls. Light AAC-block or drywall partitions wherever a wall above does not sit on a wall or beam below, for example the second-floor ensuite walls.Keeps point loads off slabs and avoids slab cracking.
Roof gardenDesign the roof slab for an extra 3–4 kN/m² of soil, plus a root-barrier waterproofing membrane, a protection screed, drainage cells and two outlets. Raised planters sit on beam lines.Leaks and overloading are the common roof-garden failures.
Codes and materialsIS 456 and IS 875 loads, IS 1893 (Bengaluru is seismic zone II) with IS 13920 detailing for the stilt floor, M25 concrete, Fe 500D/550D steel, a plinth beam, damp-proof course, anti-termite treatment and 40 mm cover at the plinth.Stilt floors are the soft storey in an earthquake, so columns need proper ductile detailing.
WaterSlope the roof and setbacks to drains. Fit a BWSSB rainwater-harvesting pit in the rear setback and a waterproofed sump in the north-east.Damp and seepage are the main long-term problems in Bengaluru homes.

5. Compromises to accept or reject

  • Bedroom 2 is compact (about 8.7 m²) and shares the common bathroom at the front of the first floor, about 6 m away. It has a queen bed and a 1.6 m wardrobe with a loft.
  • The study / guest room is gone. The second-floor lounge or the master's writing desk can take its place.
  • The kitchen is a little smaller (about 9.8 m²) because bedroom 2 took the north-east of the first floor. It keeps the island sink, east-facing hob, pantry and fridge.
  • The master ensuite is in the north-centre, a Vastu compromise. The front now holds the lounge and stair.
  • The lounge glass faces west. Use low-e glass, openable panes and blinds or bamboo chiks so it doesn't overheat on summer afternoons.
  • Ground coverage may exceed a cap. See section 4.
