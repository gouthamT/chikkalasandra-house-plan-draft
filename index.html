Stage 1, revision B6 · feasibility

Bigger kitchen, Vastu-aligned, structurally sound

The plan meets the main Vastu rules and has a simple, stackable structural frame. An architect still needs to confirm the small-plot setbacks and the ground-coverage cap, and a structural engineer must design the frame after a soil test.

Latest changes. The entrance gate and the main door are now bigger and rectangular, faced with the same vertical timber battens as the car gate: a 1.14 m double gate and a 1.06 × 2.40 m main door. The washing machine moved to a laundry nook under the stair, off the entry passage. On the first floor the TV sits between two windows, the main sofa backs onto the stair railing, and a second sofa backs onto a slim prep island that replaces the stools (the kitchen sink is now the one under the counter window). The second-floor lounge has an L-shaped sofa and no TV.

Latest kitchen change. The island now runs parallel to the cooking counter and is slimmer (0.60 m cabinet, 1.06 m aisle), with the main sink. A second sink sits at the north end of the counter under the window. The pooja room's partition walls and the shelves along the north wall are gone: the pooja is now an open mandir corner in the north-east, and the kitchen reads as one open space.

What changed in B6. The solid wall beside the stairs is gone. Each flight now has an open balustrade on its room side, with slim black-steel balusters at child-safe spacing and a timber handrail, plus a second handrail on the wall side. A skylight over the stairwell lets daylight fall through all three floors. At ground level a steel-framed glass screen keeps the stair secure behind the main door while letting light in.

The second-floor balcony is now an enclosed family lounge. Openable steel-framed glass fills both arches above a 0.45 m sill, and the lounge joins the front of the floor to make a space of about 12 m²: window seat, family sofa, TV / shelf wall and a reading nook. You step into it straight off the stair. The master bedroom gave up its front corner (now about 9.3 m²) and opens off the hall.

What changed in B5. The kitchen moved to the south-east and is about 2.5 times bigger. The hob is on the east wall so you cook facing east, the sink is in the island, and a window plus a ducted chimney on the east wall take the fumes out. Storage includes overheads, a loft, a tall pantry, a crockery unit with open shelves, and island cupboards.

The first-floor bedroom shrank into a study / guest room with a sofa-cum-bed in the north-west. A pooja room sits in the north-east and a bathroom in the north-west. The second-floor master grew to about 11.5 m², with its ensuite moved to the north-west. Every bed now sleeps head-south or head-east, and the stairs have 17 risers per floor. The plans now show the column grid, and there is a structure section below.

3. Structure

This is a preliminary scheme so the layout is buildable. A licensed structural engineer must design and sign the actual drawings after a soil test.

ElementProposalWhy
FrameRCC frame: 8 columns about 230 × 380 mm, in two rows (north and south walls) at about 3.7 m spacing, running unbroken from footing to roof.No floating columns. Every column stacks straight down. Shown on all four plans.
Beams and slabsCross beams about 230 × 450–500 mm spanning the 5.4 m width at each column line. 150 mm two-way slabs. Edge beams around the stair opening.The car porch and open first floor stay free of middle columns.
FoundationsIsolated footings for the north row. Combined or strap footings for the south row, because those columns sit on the boundary and can't spread onto the neighbour's land.Eccentric boundary footings are the usual cause of tilting and cracks on narrow plots if not tied back.
StairsRCC waist-slab flights spanning between floor and half-landing beams, carried by the south-wall columns.Each flight has the same geometry, so formwork can be reused.
Walls230 mm outer walls. Light AAC-block or drywall partitions wherever a wall above does not sit on a wall or beam below, for example the second-floor ensuite walls.Keeps point loads off slabs and avoids slab cracking.
Roof gardenDesign the roof slab for an extra 3–4 kN/m² of soil, plus a root-barrier waterproofing membrane, a protection screed, drainage cells and two outlets. Raised planters sit on beam lines.Leaks and overloading are the common roof-garden failures.
Codes and materialsIS 456 and IS 875 loads, IS 1893 (Bengaluru is seismic zone II) with IS 13920 detailing for the stilt floor, M25 concrete, Fe 500D/550D steel, a plinth beam, damp-proof course, anti-termite treatment and 40 mm cover at the plinth.Stilt floors are the soft storey in an earthquake, so columns need proper ductile detailing.
WaterSlope the roof and setbacks to drains. Fit a BWSSB rainwater-harvesting pit in the rear setback and a waterproofed sump in the north-east.Damp and seepage are the main long-term problems in Bengaluru homes.

5. Compromises to accept or reject

  • The master bedroom is smaller (about 9.3 m², with a 0.45 m deep sliding wardrobe) to make room for the family lounge.
  • The lounge glass faces west. Use low-e glass, openable panes and blinds or bamboo chiks so it doesn't overheat on summer afternoons.
  • The first-floor bedroom is now a study / guest room with a sofa-cum-bed (about 5.4 m²). That was the price of the bigger kitchen. It can take a 4 ft bed if you drop the desk.
  • The first-floor bathroom is a compact wet room (1.3 × 1.27 m) with a hand shower rather than a shower cubicle.
  • Bedroom 2 and its ensuite sit in Vastu-weaker zones (north-east and south-east). Best used as a children's room or study.
  • You still walk the length of each floor to go up again. That comes with stacked straight stairs.
  • Ground coverage may exceed a cap. See section 4.
